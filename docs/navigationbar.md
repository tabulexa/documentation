# Buttons in the navigation bar
![images](/documentation/assets/images/LeftBar.png)


## Projection window

The projection window is the window which is observable by the students. There are three buttons in the navigation panel on the left to control it. 

<img src="/documentation/assets/images/Projection.jpg" width="150" style="float: right;">


- The button at the top opens and closes the projection window.
- The window will always open on the next display in you Windows 10 display list, i.e. the display that follows after the display with Tabulexa's main window. Typically, that is the projector or a second monitor, but it can also be a television or another miracast device. Repeated pressing of the “Flip screen” button cycles the projection window through the display list.
- Press the “Fullscreen” button to start or stop the full screen mode of the projection window.

You can project your lecture to any device that can be used as an **extended screen** in in Windows 10. Of course, you can set Windows' projection mode to "duplicate", but you will not really make use of Tablexa's features, and you will probably run into problems with the screen resolution. 

**Zoom:** To share Tabulexa’s projection window in Zoom, just start “Screen sharing” in Zoom and select the projection window (not the screen). Do not expect too much. The update frequency for your handwriting will be slower than on a second screen or projector, but it still seems to be much faster than Zoom’s internal whiteboard. You can get slightly better results if you select the option “Optimize screen sharing for video clips” at the bottom of Zoom’s selection window when you activate screen sharing.

Note that Zoom does not change the aspect ratio of your shared window. I would suggest using a common ratio, such as 16:9, unless you want a lot of students to see black bars. The overall size of the projection window does not seem to effect the resolution of the video stream.

**Miracast**: You can use Miracast or other wireless display adapters to display your projection window on a television or other HDMI device. Connect Windows 10 the device using the “Connect” button in your notification panel. For the Tabulexa app, your television is just another device in your display list, and you can use the “Flip screen” button to move your projection window to the TV.



## Full screen modes

<img src="/documentation/assets/images/FullScreen.jpg" width="150" style="float: right;">
Both, the user interface and the projection window can be switched to fullscreen mode. For the projection window, press the "Fullscreen" button to start or stop the fullscreen mode. For the user interface, tap the button with the diagonal arrow. To exit the fullscreen mode, tap the middle one of your three window buttons.


**The window buttons are usually hidden in fullscreen mode, and you need to move your pen or mouse pointer to the top right corner to see them.**



## Control modes

The app has three control modes that can be selected by pressing the (D,E,R) buttons on the left toolbar.

<img src="/documentation/assets/images/Modes.jpg" width="150" style="float: right;">

- The **DIRECT (D)** mode provides the normal live-lecture experience. The students see directly what you write on the tablet or laptop.
- The **EDIT (E)** mode is intended for the preparation process. It allows you to write the text for a lecture and to group the text in blocks. You can split, join, and re-order the text blocks. 
- The **REPLAY (R)** mode is intended for a live-lecture. You can write text in the same way as in the direct mode. In addition, the pre-recorded blocks are greyed out and not visible to the students. You can activate and play them whenever needed.
