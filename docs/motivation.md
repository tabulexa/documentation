# Motivation

Handwritten lectures have always been popular in STEM subjects, and they show a recent revival in other fields. Writing on a blackboard slows down the professor and it provides enough time for the students to digest the content. In addition, writing the lecture notes themselves drastically increases the students' focus and it forces them to follow a derivation step by step. Just watch students during a typical "death by power point" presentation and compare it to the focused concentration during a maths lecture.

However, blackboards and whiteboards are no longer timely, and they are often removed from lecture rooms. Covid-19 with a demand for online teaching accelerates this process even more. This app provides a simple interface to write during a lecture and to project the handwriting to a second screen, such as a projector, television or Zoom window.

------



## Design features



**Decouple the lecturer's presentation interface from the projection window for the students:** The main reason to decouple the presentation interface is not the student's distraction due to control-buttons and flyout-menus, but it is the need to zoom in and out while writing with a digital pen. Most people seem to have a larger handwriting on a graphics tablet than on a sheet of paper. To avoid filling the complete board with just a few lines of text, it is necessary to increase the zoom factor while writing. The students, however, should only see the complete board without changes. 

<img src="/documentation/assets/images/ProjectionWindow.jpg" width="350">



**Pre-recorded handwritten blocks in combination with direct handwriting:** There are incredible educational videos with handwriting on youtube, e.g. see the Khan Academy. The presentation style and the didactic concepts in those videos are fantastic. Unfortunately, today's students started to expect a similar presentation quality during a lecture, without any chance of achieving it live (at least for me).

In addition, it is a waste of time to prepare the layout of the text for preparation, erase it, and reproduce it with the students. It is much easier to pre-record the handwritten text and replay sections of it during the lecture - whenever it is needed.
